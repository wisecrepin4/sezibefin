## Newsworthy findings

### Finding 1. Regional customers spend 38% more per order than city customers

**Headline:** Regional Australians spend 38% more per online order than city shoppers.

The numbers: regional orders averaged **$171**. Metro orders averaged **$124**. That is a **38% gap**, and it runs the opposite way to what most retailers assume, which is that the cities are where the money is.

- Regional: 25 orders, $4,275 total, $171 average
- Metro: 30 orders, $3,720 total, $124 average

Why a journalist cares: it overturns a common assumption about Australian retail, it has a clean number, and it speaks to the cost-of-living and city-versus-country conversations already running in the press.

**Strength: Strong.** The gap is large and clear. One honest note for your real pitch: this demo file holds 55 orders. Run the same analysis across your full two-year order history before you send anything, so you can quote the figure from thousands of orders, not dozens. The direction will almost certainly hold. Quote it from the big number.

### Finding 2. The big-ticket basket lives outside the cities

**Headline:** Country shoppers drive the big-ticket basket: 4WD gear sells three times harder outside the cities.

4WD and Recovery is the highest-value category in the data, averaging **$249 per order** against a site-wide average of $145. Regional customers placed **7 of the 10** such orders. 4WD gear made up **28% of regional orders** but only **10% of metro orders**.

Why a journalist cares: it gives the regional-spend story a concrete "why", and it is a clean retail-trend angle.

**Strength: Strong.**

### Finding 3. Loyalty runs deeper in the regions

**Headline:** Regional customers are the loyal ones: 60% are repeat buyers.

**60%** of regional orders came from returning customers. In the metro areas it was **40%**.

Why a journalist cares: loyalty and retention are a constant business-press theme, and "the regions are more loyal" is a fresh take.

**Strength: Moderate.** A real and clear pattern. The sample is modest, so frame it as a supporting point, not the headline.

### Finding 4. Half the year's spend lands in four months

**Headline:** More than half of annual outdoor spend happens in just four months.

**53%** of all orders fell in the September to December spring and summer window.

Why a journalist cares: it is a useful seasonality angle for retail and supply-chain coverage.

**Strength: Moderate.** Expected for outdoor retail, so it works best as colour inside a bigger story, not on its own.

## The story to lead with

Lead with **Finding 1**. It is the strongest, the most surprising, and the most quotable, and Findings 2 and 3 exist to back it up. The narrative writes itself: regional Australians spend more per order, they buy the big-ticket gear, and they come back more often. The city is not the centre of gravity that retailers assume.

## Study concept

**Working title:** Regional Australia Outspends the City: What Online Retailers Are Missing

**Summary:** A two-year analysis of online outdoor-retail orders found that customers outside Australia's capital cities spend 38% more per order than city customers, are three times more likely to buy big-ticket gear, and are markedly more loyal. The study argues that retailers who treat regional Australia as a secondary market are misreading where the value is.

**Key stat:** Regional shoppers spend 38% more per order ($171 vs $124).

**Methodology note:** Based on two years of order data, January 2024 to December 2025, segmented by metro and regional delivery location. (For the real pitch, state the full order count from your complete data set.)

**What would make it stronger:** Order counts from the full data set, a breakdown by state, and one or two named regional towns or postcodes to give reporters a local hook.

## What this skill could not do for you

Two honest limits, because this is a no-hype pack.

**The viral creative angle is still yours.** Everything above is solid and logical. It is the strong angle, well evidenced. It is not the strange, counterintuitive, perfectly-timed angle that makes a campaign genuinely go viral. That comes from a human who understands culture and timing. Use these angles as your base and push for the weird one on top.

**The journalist relationship is still yours.** This pack can write a personalised, well-targeted email. It cannot make a journalist trust you. Coverage comes faster when the reporter already knows you. Use these emails to start, and keep building the actual relationships. The skill makes you faster and better prepared. It does not replace the human connection.

## Bottom line

The strongest story in this data is the 38% regional spending premium, and the most likely first win is industry trade and regional business press, where a clean, surprising retail data point with a city-versus-country angle is exactly what editors want.
