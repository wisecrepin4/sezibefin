# Commercial Coffee Machine Lease: How It Works and What It Costs in Melbourne

Opening a cafe, fitting out a restaurant, or kitting out an office kitchen means finding several thousand dollars for an espresso machine before you have served a single coffee. Leasing is how a lot of Melbourne venues get the machine they need without that upfront hit. Here is how it works, what it costs, and how to tell whether it is the right move for your venue.

## What a commercial coffee machine lease actually is

A commercial coffee machine lease is a fixed-term agreement where you pay a regular amount, usually weekly or monthly, to use a commercial espresso machine instead of buying it outright. At the end of the term you typically have a few options: hand the machine back, upgrade to a newer one, or pay a final amount to own it.

It is closer to renting than to a loan. You get the machine working on your counter from day one, and the cost is spread across the months it is actually earning you money.

## Leasing vs buying outright vs buying second-hand

Buying outright is cheapest over the long run if you have the cash, because you pay no financing cost and the machine is an asset you own. The downside is the large upfront payment and the fact that you carry the repair bills and the risk of the machine ageing.

Buying second-hand lowers the upfront cost but raises the risk. A commercial machine that has already pulled hundreds of thousands of shots may need work sooner than you think.

Leasing spreads the cost, usually includes servicing, and often lets you upgrade. You pay more over the full term than you would buying outright, and you do not own the machine unless your lease includes a final purchase option.

## When leasing is the right call (and when it is not)

Leasing tends to suit a new venue that is watching cash flow, an owner who wants the option to upgrade as the business grows, and anyone who would rather have servicing handled than manage it themselves.

Leasing is usually the wrong call for an established venue with cash in the bank and stable coffee volumes. If you can comfortably buy outright and you know your machine size is not going to change, buying is the cheaper path.

## What to check before you sign a lease

Before you sign, check the total cost across the full term, not just the weekly figure. Check what happens at the end of the term and whether owning the machine is an option. Check whether servicing and breakdown cover are included. Check whether you can upgrade mid-term if your venue grows. And check the exit terms if you sell the business or close.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a coffee machine lease tax deductible?** Lease payments are generally a deductible business expense, but confirm your situation with your accountant.

**What happens if the machine breaks down?** With a lease that includes servicing, repairs are handled for you. Always check this is in writing.

**Can I upgrade to a bigger machine later?** Often yes, depending on the lease. If you expect to grow, ask for an upgrade clause before you sign.
